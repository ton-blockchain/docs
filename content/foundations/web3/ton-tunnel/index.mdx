TON Tunnel routes ADNL traffic through intermediate nodes. The official implementation, ADNL Tunnel, provides the relay functionality described in TON Proxy announcements.

Applications that integrate ADNL Tunnel can use a relay's network endpoint instead of communicating directly from their own endpoint.

## Clients and relays

A tunnel client selects relays from a configured pool and establishes routes for outgoing and returning traffic. Routes can contain one or more relays.

A relay runs `tunnel-node` and forwards traffic according to encrypted routing instructions. Operators can share a public configuration containing their relay's public key and optional payment terms.

TON Tunnel transports ADNL packets. Applications such as Tonutils-Proxy and tonutils-reverse-proxy integrate it with their existing networking. It does not replace the HTTP proxy described in [TON Proxy](/foundations/web3/ton-proxy).

## Privacy and scope

Routing instructions are encrypted for each relay. A relay processes its own instructions before forwarding traffic.

Relays still observe the network connections they participate in. For example, the first relay receives a connection from the tunnel client. Using a tunnel does not by itself guarantee anonymity.

Only traffic sent through the tunnel integration uses these routes. Other connections made by the application or device are outside that scope.

## Optional traffic payments

Relays can forward traffic without charging or accept payments through TON Payment Network.

Operators configure prices for forwarding traffic and for traffic entering or leaving the tunnel through a gateway. Clients use the payment terms associated with the relays in their pool.

When payments are disabled on the client, it selects only pool entries without payment requirements.
